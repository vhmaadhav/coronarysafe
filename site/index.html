Quality control for AI coronary segmentation

Find the coronary trees an AI got dangerously wrong, without ground truth.

AI models segment coronary arteries from CT angiography, and sometimes lose a main artery or break it in two while still scoring above 0.90 Dice. CoronarySafe ranks every reconstruction so that a reviewer checking a fixed 20% of scans catches the critical failures.

The problem

Dice hides the failures that matter

Segmentation models are judged by overlap. But a coronary tree can overlap the reference almost perfectly and still miss the proximal LAD, disconnect a trunk, or bridge the left and right trees. Those are the errors that change a stenosis grade or a simulated FFR.

Each dot is one AI reconstruction of a sealed test patient. Red dots are anatomically critical failures. Many sit at Dice 0.90 and above, where an overlap threshold would wave them through.

Critical, not cosmetic

A failure counts only if it threatens a large territory: a missed or broken LM, LAD, LCx or RCA within 50 mm of the ostium, a leak into a vein-sized structure, or a left–right bridge.

Pre-registered

Plan, success criteria and the single frozen model were fixed before the test set was opened. Every criterion is reported, including the one that failed.

Sealed, once

The primary test used a segmenter the system never saw, on 160 patients scored exactly once. Follow-ups ran under dated addenda.

Results

Slide the review budget

How many critical failures would a reviewer catch, checking only the top-ranked scans? Compare CoronarySafe with the best pre-specified simple rule (number of components ≥ 10 mm) and with perfect hindsight.

Computed live from the sealed per-scan scores and labels. At the pre-registered 20% budget on FFR-UNet: 29 vs 25 catches, normalised recall 0.906 vs 0.781.

The gain over the simple rule cleared the +10 pp bar but its confidence interval touched zero. Simple topology does most of the work; the learned model adds a small, consistent edge.

Robustness

Does the edge hold up?

The advantage over the simple rule held on every reconstruction set and replicated across five nnU-Net models of the same patients. When the CT itself was degraded (more noise, lower contrast, blur), the edge shrank and finally vanished under blur.

That is the honest headline: a cheap, anatomy-aware triage captures most of what is catchable, and a learned combination adds a small but fragile increment.
